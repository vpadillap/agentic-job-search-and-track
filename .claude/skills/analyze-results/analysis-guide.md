# Analysis guide

What the user's own results can and cannot tell you, and how to say it. The
numbers are small (tens of applications), so honesty about uncertainty matters
more than cleverness.

## What counts as an outcome

- An application is **resolved** once it reached interview or later, was
  closed, or got no response for longer than the no-response threshold. Until
  then it is `pending` and counts in no rate.
- "Reached interview" is the main success measure; offers are too rare to
  learn from.
- Track and dismiss decisions describe the user's preferences, not success.
  "Fit to you" learns from them and is never a chance of success.

## What you may conclude

- A difference between groups only when both have five or more cases **and**
  their ranges (`low`–`high`) do not overlap, or overlap only slightly and the
  difference repeats in another period.
- A threshold (for example, no response after day N) only from at least five
  observations behind it.
- A pattern in dismissals only from five or more dismissals with the same
  reason and trait.
- A correction pattern for a job site from five or more corrected readings on
  that site.

## What you may not conclude

- Anything from a group marked `insufficient`.
- Cause. Results show association: "postings with a tailored resume reached
  interview more often", never "the tailored resume got you interviews".
- Anything about a posting or company from a single case.
- Anything the user's resume and profile do not support. A term common in
  successful postings that is missing from the resume is a gap to mention, not
  something to add.

## Confounding: check before believing a difference

Before proposing a lesson from a difference, look for a second dimension that
explains it:

- Score: higher-scored postings get tailored resumes and cover letters more
  often. Compare resume kinds within the same score band.
- Time: the user's resume, titles, or market may have changed during the
  season. Compare the last 90 days with all time.
- Channel and site: referrals are few and different. Do not compare a referral
  rate with a job-site rate as if they were the same kind of application.
- Title: one weak title can drag down a channel or a work mode that happens to
  carry most of its postings.

If a second dimension explains the difference, say so and propose nothing, or
propose the lesson about that second dimension instead.

## Wording

- Lesson text: an action, in plain words, in the panel language. No jargon
  (no "conversion", "Brier", "regression"); say "reached interview".
- Evidence: the counts, as "X of Y" and a percentage, and the comparison
  ("23 of 23 with a score of 85 or more versus 10 of 43 between 70 and 84").
- Uncertainty: "with these few cases", "so far", "this may change as more
  outcomes arrive". Never "always" or "never" from small numbers.
- A lesson about the agent's own mistakes (corrected readings) says what to
  read more carefully, not that the site is wrong.

## Estimates and levels

- Interview-chance estimates exist only when they passed a check against the
  user's past results (predictive level). Describe them as estimates with a
  range, never as promises.
- A failed check withdraws them automatically; say that plainly if it happens
  during the review, and what is needed to try again.
- Suggestions (what to apply to next, timing, salary ask, experiments) exist
  only at the prescriptive level, after two passing checks at least 14 days
  apart.
